Analysis & Hypotheses

1. Operator Experience (Primary Factor)

JOB-1010 includes OP-106 (Singh, A.) who does not appear on any other job in the dataset. This operator logged 56.00 hours (7 records, all exactly 8.00 hours/day) at the Casting stage alone. This is likely a new or trainee operator who works slower. The Casting stage shows a 58.61-hour gap between the two jobs — accounting for virtually all the difference.

2. Higher Scrap / Rework

JOB-1010 scrapped 29 units vs. only 4 for JOB-1006. More scrap means more rework — extra hours to produce replacement units. Each scrapped unit at the Finishing stage wastes all prior labour.

3. Longer Duration / Scheduling

JOB-1010 spanned 24 calendar days vs. 18 for JOB-1006. This could indicate production interruptions, equipment downtime, or lower-priority scheduling that spread work across more days with less efficient short shifts.

Next Steps — What to Investigate

• Ask the Production Manager whether OP-106 was being trained during JOB-1010.
• Check if there were equipment issues or downtime logged during JOB-1010's period.
• Ask the Controller whether OP-106's 8.00-hour entries are actual or estimated (suspiciously round).
• Review whether JOB-1010 had material quality issues not captured in the scrap reason field.
